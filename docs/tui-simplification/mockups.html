Three simpler layouts for Marshal's TUI

One shared foundation fixes how the transcript reads. Three layouts then decide where the plan and the running work live. Every frame uses the same session: four todos, a reviewer subagent and a background test run.

  • A transcript you can followEach call sits visibly under the narration that explains it, and nothing changes shape while you read.
  • Todos always visibleThe whole plan, busy or idle, at any length.
  • Running work pinned at the bottomSubagents and jobs stay in view while they run, and look like nothing else.

Goal: simplification. Each layout is judged by what it removes.

What the frame shows

  • ▸ means five things: the running beat, the running tool, the subagent card, the plan head and the job card.
  • Running work wears the input's rail. The now bar rows and the composer share ▍.
  • The first beat has already folded to “2 tools”. Finished beats fold except the newest three, so the calls vanish when you scroll back to read them.
  • Todos are two compressed rows, shown only while a turn the list drives is running. The full list is behind Ctrl+T.
  • The subagent is drawn twice (an in-transcript card and a pinned card) and leaves a third trace when it ends.
  • The running call prints raw JSON arguments.
  • Calls hang by indentation only, each with a glyph (✎ ≡ › ◈ ⧉ ┆) to learn.

What it hides

  • The now bar ranges from 0 to 10 rows, with a one-row layout under 30 rows and an open checklist from 44, a mirror row when you scroll away, receipt and attention rows, and in-bar card expansion.
  • Ctrl+G cycles three densities on top of the folding rules.

One cause

  • Work in progress is drawn in several places at once, each with its own rules for showing, folding and shrinking. The fix is fewer places.

The beat spine

  • Head: ● and the narration, wrapped in full. Right side: owner, thought Ns, duration from 15 s.
  • Calls: one line each on ├ └, with a verb, the subject, and the result on the right. Words replace category glyphs.
  • Failure: ✗ on the head and the first three output lines under the call.
  • Long beats show their last 8 calls behind … N earlier calls, live or settled.
  • Nothing folds on its own. Enter or a click expands one line; Ctrl+G expands all. The density ladder goes.

Running work, drawn once

  • Launch: an ordinary call line whose result names it: ┆ job-1 started. It states a fact, so it never updates.
  • Running: one tinted row with a ▌ edge in its kind's colour. Under NO_COLOR the edge, glyph and id still mark it.
  • Done: the row leaves and one completion event lands in the transcript at that moment, right above the agent's reaction.

The plan

  • Shows whenever a list exists, busy or idle, any length. ✓ done, ▸ in progress, · waiting.
  • Up to 6 items in full, then a window around the active item. A finished list collapses to one row until your next prompt.

How it behaves

  • The plan's height changes only when the list changes, so the transcript does not jump.
  • Scroll up and the live beat leaves the view; the status line's spinner and timer still show the turn working.

Good

  • The plan reads like a document header and never competes with running rows for space.

Costs

  • Your eye moves between the top and bottom of the screen.
  • The plan's rows come out of the transcript even while you read old history.
  • A top strip was tried in single-stack P4, then folded into the now bar.
  • The live beat still changes in place.

Removes

  • The foundation list, the live mirror row, and the now bar planner down to the running rows.

How it behaves

  • History only grows. A beat moves from the dock into history when it settles. While you follow the bottom you cannot see the move: the beat is already right above the rule.
  • The dock is bounded at 40% of the frame and sheds in a fixed order: plan to one row, live beat to its head and current call, running rows to 3 plus +N more.
  • Scrolling up leaves the dock in place, so the live beat, running work and plan stay visible. No mirror row.
  • A streamed answer commits line by line, so only the line being written lives in the dock.

Good

  • One rule explains the whole screen, and everything live is one glance from where you type.
  • Settled text never reflows: selection stays put and the render cache draws each settled node once.
  • The wire can become append-only history plus one live payload; the band node already ships separately.

Costs

  • A busy dock can take 10 or more rows, bounded at 40%.
  • When you have scrolled up, settled beats appear as history growing below you.
  • Line-by-line commit of streamed text is new work.

Removes

  • The foundation list, plus every now-bar variant (compact and tall layouts, mirror row, in-bar expansion, attention and receipt rows) and the live/settled split in the render cache.

What you are looking at

  • The plan block was born at the todo.write above the view, so it sticks to the top.
  • The two cards were born at their launch lines below the view, so they stick to the bottom.
  • The live beat is out of view, so a mirror row stands in for it.

Good

  • Each thing has one home, and the scrollback shows the plan and cards where they happened.

Costs

  • Objects move between their home and an edge as you scroll and as output arrives.
  • A finished card settles back at its launch point, usually out of view, so a completion event is still needed.
  • It needs a mirror row and a new sticky layout pass, and has the most rendering code of the three.

Considered and rejected

  • A side rail from 120 columns. Running work leaves the bottom, it needs a second layout below the breakpoint, and the rail was removed in single-stack P1 for half-width terminals.
  • A transcript sectioned by todo. Built in single-stack P3 and P4 and reverted by transcript-beats, because models do not keep todo state reliably and the structure came out wrong.
  • A flat stream, one bullet per call. Simple, but calls link to narration only by adjacency.

Decisions for you

  1. Layout: Console, Ledger or Sticky. Recommended: Console.
  2. Remove auto-folding and the density ladder? Recommended: yes; keep per-line expand and Ctrl+G expand-all.
  3. A finished plan: one row until your next prompt, or hidden at once. Recommended: one row.
  4. Failed background work: is the red completion event enough, or keep live work's attention row and acknowledgement? Recommended: the event plus the Work view; drop acknowledgement.
  5. Narration: whole, or a first-sentence headline with a muted continuation. Recommended: whole.

Suggested order

  1. Renderer foundation: spine, verbs, glyphs, no auto-fold, one level of detail. TUI only, visible on its own.
  2. Lifecycle in viewmodel: completion events replace in-place cards and tail markers. TUI and web.
  3. Layout: the chosen layout replaces nowbar.go, plus the plan surface and Ctrl+T.
  4. Wire: settle the layout before daemon P2's transcript plan, since Console changes what the wire carries.
